3  Geometry of the representation

Let h(x) denote the activation of the penultimate layer for an input x, with d = 4096 throughout. We are interested not in the span of these vectors but in the shape of the set they occupy. Following Facco et al., we estimate the intrinsic dimension from the ratio of each point's second- and first-nearest-neighbour distances, which uses only the two closest points to each sample.

We hypothesise that the representation lies on a low-dimensional manifold embedded in the ambient space, and that the intrinsic dimension k ≪ d is stable across the later layers. Figure 2 supports the second half of this claim: from layer 18 onward the estimate settles between 22 and 26 while d is unchanged.

A linear baseline is instructive. PCA retaining 95% of the variance overestimates k by a factor of three to six on every model we tested (Table 2), which we take as evidence that the manifold is curved rather than a subspace. The gap widens with depth.
